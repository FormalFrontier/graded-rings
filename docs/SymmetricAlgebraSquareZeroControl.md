# A square-zero control for the symmetric algebra of the conormal module

## Objects and the canonical map

Let `R` be an arbitrary commutative ring and `I` an ideal. Throughout, keep the original objects

- `B = reesAlgebra I`, with its inclusion in `R[t]`;
- `A = R ⧸ I`;
- `C = B ⧸ I.map (algebraMap R B)`, with its existing canonical `A`-algebra structure;
- `E₁ = reesAlgebra.powerQuotient I 1`, with its existing `A`-module structure.

In particular, the precise coefficient-quotient carrier of `E₁` is

`↥(I ^ 1) ⧸ (I • (⊤ : Submodule R ↥(I ^ 1)))`.

The top submodule here is a submodule of **the module `↥(I ^ 1)`**, not a submodule of `R`. Under the usual identification `I ^ 1 = I`, this is the conormal module `I/I²`: the image in `R` of the denominator is `I²`. Replacing that top submodule by top in `R` would produce the wrong quotient.

The canonical map is given by the following type and expression:

```lean
q₁ : E₁ →ₗ[A] C :=
  (reesAlgebra.quotientMonomial I 1).extendScalarsOfSurjective
    Ideal.Quotient.mk_surjective

Φ : SymmetricAlgebra A E₁ →ₐ[A] C :=
  SymmetricAlgebra.lift q₁
```

Here `A`, `C`, and `E₁` abbreviate only the original objects above. Scalar extension changes the linearity annotation, not the underlying function: for every `v : E₁`, `q₁ v = reesAlgebra.quotientMonomial I 1 v`. It creates no module or algebra structure. In particular it does not make the quotient map `B → C` into an `A`-map.

The characteristic formulas are

1. `Φ (SymmetricAlgebra.ι A E₁ v) = reesAlgebra.quotientMonomial I 1 v` for every `v`;
2. `(reesAlgebra.augmentation I).comp Φ = SymmetricAlgebra.algebraMapInv`, where the right side has type `SymmetricAlgebra A E₁ →ₐ[A] A` and is exactly the stock lift of the zero linear map;
3. `Function.Surjective Φ`.

Stock `SymmetricAlgebra.algHom_ext` makes the first formula a uniqueness characterization among `A`-algebra homomorphisms. For surjectivity, the range of `Φ` contains the range of `q₁`, whose underlying set is the original `(reesAlgebra.quotientHomogeneousComponent I 1 : Set C)`. The existing generation theorem says its `A`-adjoin is top. Thus the algebra range of `Φ` is top. The augmentation formula follows by the same stock extensionality: both composites kill all degree-one generators and preserve coefficient scalars.

None of these general statements needs finite generation, a chosen basis, Noetherianity, regularity, or nontriviality. The linear generator map is not a choice of representatives or a choice of basis.

## A concrete nonzero ring and ideal

Fix `k = ℚ`. Take the stock model

`R = TrivSqZeroExt k k`.

In coordinate notation, its elements are pairs `(a,b)` and multiplication is

`(a,b)(c,d) = (ac, ad + bc)`.

Write `ε = TrivSqZeroExt.inr (1 : k) = (0,1)`, and let `I = Ideal.span ({ε} : Set R)`. Then

- `ε ≠ 0`, by its second coordinate;
- `ε² = 0`;
- `I = { (0,b) | b ∈ k } = ker(fst)`;
- `I² = 0`.

Indeed, `(a,b)ε = (0,a)`, so the principal ideal generated by `ε` consists exactly of all pairs with first coordinate zero. Products of any two such pairs vanish. This identifies `I` with the stock `TrivSqZeroExt.kerIdeal k k`, but it does not change the ideal in the control.

The first-coordinate homomorphism factors through the original quotient `A = R/I`, giving a ring isomorphism `A ≃ k`, with inverse `a ↦ [(a,0)]`. In particular `A` is nontrivial: a hypothetical equality `1 = 0` in `A` would map to `1 = 0` in `ℚ`. No field instance on the Lean type `A` is required below.

## The original power quotient and an explicit coordinate functional

Because every product of two elements of `I` is zero, the ideal action of `I` on the original module `↥(I ^ 1)` is zero. Consequently

`I • (⊤ : Submodule R ↥(I ^ 1)) = ⊥`.

This follows directly from the generators of the ideal-smul submodule: each is a product of two elements of `I`, and hence is zero. Thus the original quotient `E₁` has a distinguished element

`e = [ε] ∈ E₁`,

which is nonzero. We do not replace `E₁` by a new carrier or give it another action.

Define an explicit functional on representatives by

`λ([(0,b)]) = [(b,0)] ∈ A`.

It is well-defined on the original power quotient because its denominator is zero. It is additive. For scalar compatibility, represent a scalar of `A` by `r = (a,c) ∈ R`; then

`r · (0,b) = (0,ab)`

and

`λ([r · (0,b)]) = [(ab,0)] = [(a,c)] [(b,0)]` in `A`.

The last equality holds because `(a,c)(b,0) - (ab,0) = (0,cb) ∈ I`. This proves compatibility with the **original** quotient `A`-action. Equivalently, first regard this as an `R`-linear map into `A`, and use surjectivity of `R → A` to promote it to `λ : E₁ →ₗ[A] A` without changing its function. In particular `λ(e) = [(1,0)] = 1`.

This functional is specified by coordinates of the concrete square-zero extension, not by choosing a basis. It is only a detector used to prove a property of this example. It is not input to the general map `Φ`.

## Direct control of the original target quotient

Since `I² = 0`, an element of the original Rees algebra `B` is uniquely a polynomial

`r + m t`, with `r ∈ R` and `m ∈ I`.

All coefficients of degree at least two vanish, since they lie in `Iⁿ = 0` for `n ≥ 2`. Let `J = I.map (algebraMap R B)`, exactly the ideal in the definition of `C`.

**Claim.** `J` consists exactly of the constant polynomials with value in `I`.

For one inclusion, its ideal generators are precisely constants from `I`. Multiplying such a constant `i` by `r + m t` gives `ir + im t = ir`, since `im = 0`. Every finite sum of such products is therefore a constant from `I`. Conversely every constant from `I` is one of the generators, hence belongs to `J`.

Define

`η = [ε t] ∈ C`.

Then `η ≠ 0`: membership of `ε t` in `J` would force its degree-one coefficient to be zero, whereas that coefficient is `ε ≠ 0`. Also

`η² = [(ε t)²] = [ε² t²] = 0`.

By the existing representative law `quotientMonomial_mk`, the original degree-one map takes `e` to exactly `η`. Thus, for `z = SymmetricAlgebra.ι A E₁ e`, the canonical map has the concrete value

`Φ(z) = η ≠ 0`, and `Φ(z)² = 0`.

This identifies the value in the defining quotient, not in a substituted target. The equivalence `quotientMonomialEquiv` identifies `E₁` with the degree-one additive subgroup, and `coe_quotientMonomialEquiv` identifies its ambient value with `quotientMonomial`. Its injectivity therefore also detects that the image of `e` is nonzero.

The surjectivity statement is nonvacuous in this example. If `r = (a,b)` and `m = (0,c)`, the class of `r + m t` in `C` is

`algebraMap A C ([(a,0)]) + algebraMap A C ([(c,0)]) * η`.

This is the image under `Φ` of the corresponding scalar plus scalar multiple of `z`. This elementary computation explains the generic generation/range proof, but is not a replacement definition of `C` or of its `A`-algebra structure.

## A nonzero square in the source

In the ordinary polynomial algebra `A[X]`, define the `A`-linear map

`h : E₁ →ₗ[A] A[X]`, with `h(v) = λ(v) • X = C(λ(v)) X`.

Its linearity follows from the linearity of `λ` and distributivity of scalar multiplication on `A[X]`. Form the stock lift

`Θ = SymmetricAlgebra.lift h : SymmetricAlgebra A E₁ →ₐ[A] A[X]`.

Then `Θ(z) = X`, since `λ(e) = 1`, and therefore `Θ(z²) = X²`. Because `A` is nontrivial, `X² ≠ 0`: its coefficient at degree two is `1`, or equivalently use `monomial 2 1 = X²` together with `monomial 2 1 = 0 ↔ 1 = 0`.

Consequently `z² ≠ 0`. But `Φ(z²) = η² = 0`. Thus the original canonical map is **not injective in general**, even though its degree-one generator image is nonzero and it is surjective. We have exhibited one nonzero kernel element only; no full kernel presentation is asserted or needed. No isomorphism from the symmetric algebra to a polynomial ring, no basis, and no new grading/kernel engine is used.

## Augmentation and boundary behavior

The original augmentation sends `η` to zero by its positive-degree vanishing law and sends `1` to `1 ≠ 0`. Thus neither the target nor its augmentation is vacuous. Its composite with `Φ` sends `z` to zero while preserving coefficient scalars, exactly as the stock zero evaluation `SymmetricAlgebra.algebraMapInv` does.

For `I = ⊥`, the original `E₁` is zero, the Rees algebra has only constants, and `C ≃ R = A`; the canonical map is the scalar map from the symmetric algebra of the zero module, hence an isomorphism. For `I = ⊤`, the original quotient `A` is the zero ring, `J = ⊤` because it contains `1`, `C` is the zero ring, and the original `E₁ = R/R` is zero. The source is also a zero ring, and the unique map is surjective.
